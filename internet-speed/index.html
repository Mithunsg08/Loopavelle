How to read your speed test results

A quick guide to the numbers and how to get the most accurate test.

What each metric means

  • Download – how fast data reaches you (streaming, browsing, downloads).
  • Upload – how fast you send data (video calls, backups, uploads).
  • Ping – round-trip delay in milliseconds. Lower is better.
  • Jitter – variation in ping. Below 20 ms is ideal.
  • Packet loss – share of probes that never returned. Aim for 0%.
  • Loaded latency – ping while your line is busy. Reveals bufferbloat.

Tips for an accurate test

  • Use a wired Ethernet connection when possible.
  • Pause downloads, streams and cloud sync on all devices.
  • Disconnect your VPN or proxy.
  • Close other browser tabs and restart your router occasionally.
  • Run the test 2–3 times at different hours and compare.

Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about testing your internet speed.

What is a good internet speed?

For most households 25–100 Mbps download is enough for HD streaming, video calls and browsing. 4K streaming needs about 25 Mbps per stream, and busy homes with many devices benefit from 200 Mbps or more.

What are ping and jitter?

Ping (latency) is the time in milliseconds a small packet takes to reach a server and return. Jitter is how much that time varies. Low ping and low jitter matter most for gaming, video calls and live streaming.

What is bufferbloat?

Bufferbloat is the extra delay your connection suffers when it is busy downloading or uploading. A large increase in latency under load causes lag in calls and games even when your raw speed looks high.

Why is my internet speed lower than what I pay for?

Wi-Fi interference, distance from the router, many connected devices, old hardware, ISP congestion at peak hours and VPNs can all reduce speed. Test with a wired connection and with other devices paused to see your real line speed.

Is the Internet Speed test accurate?

It uses several parallel connections to a nearby global edge network and ignores the first moments of the transfer, which is the same approach used by professional tools. Results vary with your browser, device and network load, so run it a few times.

Does the test use a lot of data?

A full test can use from 50 MB on slow connections to several hundred MB on fast ones. Use a shorter duration if you are on a metered or mobile data plan.
