      Why people use Image Compress

      Many forms, job portals, exam sites and email clients cap uploads at a fixed size, such as 20 KB for a signature or 200 KB for a photo. Image Compress lets you hit that limit directly instead of guessing at quality sliders.

      Because the work happens in your browser, private documents, IDs and personal videos are never sent to a server. It also keeps working on slow connections once the page has loaded.

      Frequently asked questions

      Are my files uploaded to a server?

      No. Compression uses your own device through your browser. Your images and videos never leave it.

      What is the smallest size I can set?

      0.5 KB. Anything lower is not processed.

      How do I compress an image to 100 KB?

      Add the image, type 100 in the size box and press Compress. The tool lowers quality first and shrinks dimensions only if it must.

      Can I compress a video to a specific size?

      Yes. The video is re-encoded in your browser at a bitrate worked out from your target. It takes about as long as the video plays, so keep the tab open. The final size lands close to your target.

      Why is my result a different format?

      Browsers compress images as JPG or WebP, and videos as WebM or MP4, depending on what your browser supports.
